Project · AI for a small business

A weekly lead list for local contractors, built from public records with AI

Every night it reads the agendas of 36 village boards and zoning commissions in Lake County, Illinois, and every Monday it hands a contractor a short, ranked list of businesses that are about to need a build-out, months before a permit is pulled.

See the code, the ranking rules and a sample report on GitHub →

I wanted to keep up with development and land-use decisions in the towns around me, but it meant digging through dozens of village websites and PDFs. Small contractors have the same problem and no time to solve it, including my family's contracting business, which this is built for.

How I built it with AI

  1. Started from the customer's question, not the data. I researched what general contractors actually look for in leads (project size, owner-operators, timing) and turned that into the A/B/C rules.
  2. Described the job in plain English; Claude Code wrote the pipeline. It found each village's agenda system, wrote the collectors and the PDF extraction, and wrote tests for each piece.
  3. Made the judgment calls myself. Which towns matter, what counts as a lead, what to leave out (houses, renewals, public works), and how the report should read for a busy contractor.
  4. Iterated on real output. The first draft report flagged about 160 items, most of them noise. Reading it line by line with Claude and tightening the rules cut it to a short list: 18 leads a contractor would act on in the first weekly report.
  5. Built in privacy from the start. Agendas name homeowners who apply for a variance, so the reports withhold private people's names and home addresses. Company names and official decisions stay.
  6. Made it run without me. It runs nightly with no servers, checks itself with tests before every run, and flags itself red when something breaks.

How it works

Runs on a schedule with no servers to maintain. It is polite to village websites (one request per second, robots.txt honored) and checks itself with automated tests before each run.

What's public and what's private

Public: the code, the ranking rules, the tests and a sample report. Private: the archive of agendas, minutes and packets it has collected.

Every document comes from a public village website, but many of them also name residents: homeowners asking for a variance, people who spoke at a meeting, sometimes with their home address or phone number. Each one is public on its own, buried in one village's PDF. Gathered into one searchable collection, they would make it easy to look people up, and that isn't what the villages published them for. So the leads show businesses and official decisions, and the raw records stay private.
